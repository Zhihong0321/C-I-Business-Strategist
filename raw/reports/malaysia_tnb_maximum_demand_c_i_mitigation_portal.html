The Regulatory Period 4 (RP4) Paradigm Shift

Context & Overview: On 1 July 2025, *Suruhanjaya Tenaga* (Energy Commission) and Tenaga Nasional Berhad (TNB) implemented Regulatory Period 4 (RP4), governing Peninsular Malaysia's electricity tariffs through 31 December 2027. RP4 abolished legacy activity-based tariff codes (Tariffs B, C1, C2, D, E1, E2, E3) in favor of voltage-categorized Non-Domestic accounts (Low Voltage, Medium Voltage, High Voltage). This section explores how RP4 rebalanced utility cost recovery by slashing per-kWh energy rates while dramatically elevating Maximum Demand (kW) charges by up to 202%.

Key Pillars of the RP4 Tariff Rebalancing

  • ►
    Unbundled Capacity & Network Charges: Legacy bundled MD charges (RM30.30–RM45.10/kW) were replaced by two separate grid infrastructure components: a Capacity Charge (generation reserve reservation) and a Network Charge (transmission/distribution line capacity), pushing Medium Voltage peak demand rates to RM89.27/kW (General) and RM97.06/kW (Time-of-Use).
  • ►
    Compressed Weekday Peak Window (14:00 – 22:00): RP4 compressed the ToU Peak Period from the legacy 14-hour block (08:00–22:00) down to an 8-hour window on weekdays only. Off-peak hours now encompass 128 hours per week (76.2% of all hours), including the entire 08:00–14:00 morning shift and all weekend hours.
  • ►
    Monthly Automatic Fuel Adjustment (AFA): Replaced the legacy 6-month ICPT cycle with a dynamic monthly fuel pass-through, transferring global coal/gas price fluctuations directly onto monthly C&I energy bills.

Physics & Mathematical Formulation of TNB MD

Context & Mathematical Mechanics: TNB does not bill Maximum Demand on instantaneous microsecond current spikes or motor starting inrush currents. Instead, TNB defines Maximum Demand as twice the largest active energy (kWh) imported during any consecutive 30-minute block in the billing month. This section presents the exact mathematical formulas governing interval demand, power factor penalties, and peak window integration.

1. 30-Min Interval Integration
Di (kW) = 2 × Ei (kWh)

In any 30-minute block $i$ ($t = 0.5\text{ h}$), active energy $E_i = M_{\text{end}} - M_{\text{start}}$ is integrated. Multiplying by 2 converts 0.5-hour energy into average active power demand in kW.

1,440 blocks per 30-day billing month
2. Monthly Billed MD Lock-In
MDbilled = maxi ∈ W (2 × Ei)

TNB scans all assessment blocks $W$. A single 30-minute interval where consumption reaches $2,000\text{ kWh}$ sets a $4,000\text{ kW}$ demand charge ($\text{RM}388,240/\text{month}$ on MV ToU) for the entire billing cycle.

W = 14:00–22:00 weekdays for ToU
3. Power Factor Surcharge Formula
PF = kWh / √(kWh² + kVArh²)

If monthly average PF drops below $0.85$ (LV/MV) or $0.90$ (HV), TNB levies a penalty: $1.5\%$ surcharge per $0.01$ drop down to $0.75$, plus $3.0\%$ per $0.01$ below $0.75$.

Threshold: 0.85 (LV/MV), 0.90 (HV)

Zero-CapEx Tariff Switch: MV General vs MV ToU Decision Boundary

Switching from Medium Voltage General (RM89.27/kW) to Medium Voltage ToU (RM97.06/kW) introduces a demand penalty of RM7.79/kW/month. To justify the switch, the off-peak energy savings ($2.60\text{ sen/kWh}$ discount) or demand reduction outside 14:00–22:00 must outweigh this differential.

Governing C&I Arbitrage Inequality:
Etotal × (0.0409 × α - 0.0149) + 89.27 × MD24h - 97.06 × MDpeak > 0

Scenario A: Unchanged Peak Location (α constraint)

If peak demand cannot be shifted out of the 14:00–22:00 window ($\text{MD}_{\text{peak}} = \text{MD}_{24\text{h}}$), the facility must consume at least α > 36.43% + (190.46 × MD / Etotal) of its energy during off-peak hours to break even.

Example: 500k kWh/mo with 1,000 kW peak requires > 74.5% off-peak energy share.

Scenario B: Peak Demand Shifted Outside Peak Window

If process rescheduling reduces peak demand during 14:00–22:00 by just > 8.03% relative to morning/night demand ($\text{MD}_{\text{peak}} < 0.9197 \times \text{MD}_{24\text{h}}$), ToU becomes immediately profitable even at neutral energy splits.

Immediate savings on all morning & weekend demand spikes (billed at RM0/kW).

Operational Demand-Side Management & Process Sequencing

Operational DSM eliminates simultaneous concurrency of high-draw machinery during the 14:00–22:00 weekday peak window without requiring capital-intensive energy storage assets.

1. Morning Shift Pull-Down (06:00 – 14:00)

Because electricity demand recorded prior to 14:00 carries zero MD charge on RP4 ToU, batch furnaces, autoclaves, extruders, and thermal pull-downs should be scheduled between 06:00 and 13:30, transitioning to low-draw holding modes before 14:00.

2. Hard-Wired PLC Motor Staggering

Configure Programmable Logic Controllers (PLCs) with mutual-exclusion interlocks. Preventing three $50\text{ kW}$ air compressors from starting together in the same 30-min block saves $150\text{ kW}$, yielding RM 14,559/month in MD savings on MV ToU.

3-Tier EMS Architecture & Real-Time Trajectory Prediction

Manual operational discipline fails over time. Deploying real-time Energy Management Systems (EMS) synchronized with TNB's 30-minute integration windows prevents accidental peak lock-ins through predictive algorithms.

Real-Time Interval Peak Projection Formula:
D_projected (kW) = 2 × [ E(t_e) + P_inst × ((30 - t_e) / 60) ]
Where $t_e$ is elapsed interval minutes, $E(t_e)$ is cumulative kWh, and $P_{\text{inst}}$ is live kW draw. If projected demand exceeds target MD, the EMS automatically sheds non-critical AHUs or unloads chillers for 15 minutes.

Solar PV vs. Solar + BESS: Overcoming the Cloud Transient & Evening Peak Trap

Standalone rooftop Solar PV fails to guarantee Maximum Demand reduction on MV/HV tariffs due to afternoon cloud transients (sudden solar drops) and the 18:00–22:00 dark peak. Pairing solar with BESS secures peak shaving and avoids cheap SMP export loss (RM0.1881/kWh).

Thermal Energy Storage (TES) & Dedicated TES Tariff Optimization

For commercial complexes, hospitals, and cleanroom factories, central chillers represent up to 60% of peak electrical demand. TES systems freeze ice or chill water during off-peak overnight hours (22:00–14:00) and discharge cooling during the 14:00–22:00 weekday window, enabling chiller compressors to shut down during high-tariff periods.

TNB Dedicated MV TES Tariff Incentive:

Qualifying facilities utilizing thermal storage can apply for TNB's Medium Voltage TES tariff schedule, permanently shielding chiller compressor kilowatts from weekday peak demand surcharges.

2. GITA Asset Tax Shield Transition

Budget 2027 Shift

Budget 2027 restructured the Green Investment Tax Allowance (GITA). BESS and integrated solar storage maintain Tier 1 status through 2030, while standalone energy efficiency equipment loses eligibility after 31 Dec 2026.
